# How is this different from learning?

Creators can look up knowledge, read lectures and record mastery just like learners. They take on one more task: organizing their own understanding of mathematics for themselves or others to read.

When learning, you can follow a lecture's sequence. When creating, you decide what comes first, where examples are needed and which derivations deserve another step of explanation. You can write a complete course or focus on a single topic. You do not have to be a teacher to begin.

MakiMath's public knowledge library provides shared knowledge content. When writing a lecture, you can reference its definitions, theorems and formulas directly, then add your own explanations. A reference does not become an independently editable copy. If the shared content has a problem, it needs to be corrected through knowledge library maintenance. If you simply want to explain it differently, write your explanation before or after the reference.

The same knowledge can appear in different lectures; what differs is how authors organize and explain it. Readers keep their existing mastery records rather than starting over when they switch authors.

Local authoring does not require signing in, so learners can try it at any time. Saving lectures on the platform requires the appropriate online authoring permission, and public publication also requires review and approval.

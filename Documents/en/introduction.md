# Introduction

## 👋 What is MakiMath?

MakiMath is a platform for learning mathematics and creating lectures, built around a shared knowledge library.

The platform maintains knowledge points centrally, while different creators organize their own lectures. The same definition, theorem or formula can be referenced by multiple lectures, all drawing on the same content in the knowledge library.

Teachers can arrange knowledge points in their own teaching order and add explanations, examples and learning suggestions. Students can organize their own lectures too. Different lectures can therefore explain things differently while sharing consistent referenced knowledge.

We hope mathematical knowledge can be reused, and that the work people put into organizing knowledge and courses can give others a starting point.

## 📖 What can I do on MakiMath?

You can start learning from a lecture, or look up a knowledge point directly in the library, read its content and prerequisites, and revisit foundations you are not yet familiar with.

After learning a knowledge point, you can mark it as mastered within a lecture. This mark belongs to the knowledge point in the library, rather than just that passage in the current lecture. When you read another lecture that references the same knowledge point, it will also appear as mastered.

You can also write your own lectures: create chapters and sections, reference the content you need from the library, and add your own understanding and explanations. Referenced public knowledge points are maintained centrally in the library. You can change how your lecture is organized without repeatedly copying and maintaining the same definitions and formulas.

You can write local lectures without signing in and export them to keep a copy. With online authoring permission, you can submit your lectures for review and share them publicly once approved.

Mastery records are currently stored in your browser and shared across lectures, but do not yet sync across devices.

## 🌱 How does MakiMath differ from other mathematics learning platforms?

MakiMath's main distinction is that different lectures share the same knowledge library.

Usually, each textbook or lecture writes out its own definitions, theorems and formulas. In another book, their order, numbering and wording may change, leaving you to work out what you have already learned and what is simply being explained differently.

In MakiMath, referenced knowledge points have a shared source. The same knowledge point remains the same library entry, however many lectures it appears in. When its content in the library is updated, references in those lectures also read the updated content.

Creators differ in how they organize and explain the material: what comes first, which examples to use, and how to explain a difficult point. Each creator can make these choices without forcing every course into the same style.

Your mastery records follow the knowledge points too. Moving to another lecture does not mean starting over. Material you have mastered remains marked, so you can focus on unfamiliar content and new explanations.

This is what MakiMath hopes to achieve: help teachers avoid repeatedly organizing the same foundational knowledge, help students avoid checking what they have already learned in every lecture, and preserve different ways of teaching and learning mathematics.

## 🤝 Is MakiMath open source?

Yes. MakiMath's frontend, backend and documentation are all open source under AGPL-3.0. You can view the source code, contribute improvements, and modify and self-host the platform under the terms of the license. Lecture content is subject to the license specified by its author.

Students and teachers are welcome to participate. Whether you report issues, organize knowledge, write lectures or contribute code, your contributions can help make MakiMath better.
